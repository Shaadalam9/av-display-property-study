# Planned-path / trajectory and intention visualisations for AV occupants: display properties, levels and effects

Scope: in-vehicle displays (AR HUD / windshield display (WSD), central screen) that show the occupant what the automated vehicle (AV) plans to do. External HMIs for pedestrians are out of scope.
Verification note: items marked **[full text read]** were checked against the paper text (PDF or arXiv HTML). Items marked **[abstract/metadata only]** were checked only against the abstract, a search snippet or a reference list. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed.

## Q1. Which properties of a planned-path / trajectory overlay have been studied, at which levels, and with what effects?

### Takeaway
Few papers vary path properties one at a time. The best-controlled evidence comes from the Ulm group (Colley, Rukzio, Jansen). Those papers vary: presence of the ego path (on/off), shape/width (single line vs double line at vehicle-edge width vs Bézier cone), colour gradient (blue-to-red certainty) vs none, transparency/fade vs none, and, in Jansen et al. (2025), continuous visibility, alpha (0.1–1) and size (0.1–0.6). Effects on trust from simply showing the ego path are inconsistent: one study found lower trust and a later one found higher trust. Shape, colour and transparency had no effect on trust, workload or perceived safety, but they did affect the SART "understanding" subscale. No study found here varied path length/look-ahead horizon, animation of the path or carpet-vs-line style one at a time.

### Cited Findings

**Presence of the ego (planned) trajectory, on vs off**
- Colley, Rädler, Glimmann & Rukzio (2022), "Effects of Scene Detection, Scene Prediction, and Maneuver Planning Visualizations on Trust, Situation Awareness, and Cognitive Load in Highly Automated Vehicles", Proc. ACM IMWUT 6(2), Art. 49, DOI 10.1145/3534609. **[full text read]**
  - Design: online, between-subjects, video-based study with N=216 Prolific participants (recruited 241, excluded 25). It used a 2×2×2 design (Situation Detection × Situation Prediction × Maneuver Planning, each shown or not shown) on a pre-recorded real-world video with a simulated AR windshield, filmed from the front-passenger viewpoint. — [Colley et al. 2022 PDF](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.100/institut/Papers/Prof_Rukzio/2022/IMWUT2022_functional_hierarchy_final.pdf)
  - Ego trajectory style: "successive arrows" with a blue-to-pink colour gradient (bluer = more certain), shown directly in front of the ego vehicle. This was the same style used for other vehicles' predicted trajectories. — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609)
  - Trust: there was a significant main effect of Maneuver Planning (χ²(1)=10.12, p=0.001). Trust was **higher without** the maneuver trajectory (M=3.54, SD=.99) than with it (M=3.11, SD=.99). — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609)
  - SA (SART): no main effect of maneuver planning. There was a Detection × Maneuver Planning interaction on SART Understanding (χ²(1)=3.98, p=0.046), plus a three-way interaction on perceived safety (χ²(1)=6.09, p=0.014). Clarity of reasons for the AV's behaviour dropped with the ego trajectory when no detection was shown, and rose when detection was shown (χ²(1)=5.15, p=0.023). — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609)
  - Clutter and workload were driven by the *prediction* layer (other road users' trajectories), not by the ego path. Prediction raised visual clutter (M=4.16 vs 2.53, χ²(1)=37.48, p<0.001) and mental workload (M=12.40 vs 9.89, χ²(1)=10.78, p=0.001). — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609)
  - Authors' recommendation: show all three levels combined, and the user "should then be able to turn these on or off selectively". This is an explicit argument for user control/personalisation. — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609)
- Colley, Speidel, Strohbeck, Rixen, Belz & Rukzio (2023, issue dated Dec 2023; often cited as 2024), "Effects of Uncertain Trajectory Prediction Visualization in Highly Automated Vehicles on Trust, Situation Awareness, and Cognitive Load", Proc. ACM IMWUT 7(4), Art. 153, DOI 10.1145/3631408. **[full text read]**
  - Study 2: online, video-based study of a real-world recorded ride using a real prototype's prediction/planning output, N=238. It was a 2×2 design (own planned trajectory on/off × other vehicles' predicted trajectories on/off). Groups: baseline 54, ego only 62, prediction only 61, both 61. The ego path was a line with a blue-to-red colour gradient. — [Colley et al. 2023 PDF](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.100/1-hci/hci-paper/2023/IMWUT_2023_Uncertainty_Trajectory_final.pdf)
  - Trust was **higher with** the own planned trajectory (M=3.71, SD=0.91) than without it (M=3.41, SD=1.00): ART F(1,152)=8.61, p=0.004, ηp²=0.03. — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
  - SART Understanding was higher with the ego trajectory (M=14.57 vs 13.76; F(1,234)=4.07, p=0.045). "Clarity of next actions" was also higher with it (M=5.10 vs 4.90; F(1,234)=5.62, p=0.019). — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
  - Mental workload showed no significant effect. Perceived safety was borderline (p=0.05). Other vehicles' predicted trajectories improved perceived longitudinal and lateral control. — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
  - Open feedback: one participant said the colour-coded uncertainty on the ego path "made them doubt" the AV's ability. The authors note the display might make some users "nervous". — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
- **Conflict:** across the two Ulm studies, showing the ego path lowered trust in one (2022, arrows plus a gradient in a busy multi-layer scene) and raised it in the other (2023, a coloured line). Jansen et al. (2025) summarises the earlier work as "Visualizing trajectory planning can boost trust but may raise cognitive load". — [Jansen et al. 2025, arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)

**Shape / width (line vs vehicle-width double line vs cone), colour, transparency**
- Colley et al. 2023, Study 1: online, video-based Unity simulation, N=280. It used a 3×2×2 between-subjects design plus a baseline (13 conditions). Factors were shape (line / double line marking the vehicle edges / Bézier cone), colour (blue near the car = certain to red at the end = uncertain, vs no colour) and transparency (solid fading out with distance, vs none). The authors dropped size as a variable because internal tests showed it "quickly led to visual clutter". **[full text read]** — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
  - There were no significant effects on trust (Kruskal–Wallis χ²(12)=13.11, p=0.36), perceived safety (p=0.65) or mental workload (p=0.75). No visualization differed from the no-visualization baseline. — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
  - Transparency **lowered** SART Understanding (F(1,247)=4.18, p=0.042; also F(1,247)=7.57, p=0.006 on the Understanding measure). The authors concluded that transparency "was inappropriate in conveying uncertainty information" and dropped it. — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
  - Shape: a single **line** gave better SART Understanding (M=14.44) than the Bézier cone (M=12.93; p_adj=0.08) and the double line (M=12.95; p_adj=0.01), with F(2,247)=6.32, p=0.002. One participant said the double line was "easily mistakable for headlights". — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
  - Colour (hue gradient) was chosen as the best uncertainty carrier, because it aided understanding and lowered SART demand. "Two participants deemed the predictions too short", but length was not manipulated: it scaled with velocity in all conditions. — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
  - The authors suggest that chevrons could add directional information and stop lines could help. Participants asked for discrete-state cues (turning, stopping), which the authors had omitted. — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
- Kunze, Summerskill, Marshall & Filtness (2018), "Augmented Reality Displays for Communicating Uncertainty Information in Automated Driving", AutomotiveUI '18, pp. 164–175, DOI 10.1145/3239060.3239074. N=46 participants sorted and ranked 11 visual variables for an AR uncertainty display. Colour- and animation-based variables, especially **hue**, conveyed an urgency ordering most clearly and were well received. **[abstract/metadata only]**: the full PDF was blocked, so I could not confirm which AR element (path vs other) carried the variables. — [Loughborough repository](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/Augmented_reality_displays_for_communicating_uncertainty_information_in_automated_driving/9338876/files/16947521.pdf); [IGI journal version 2019](https://www.igi-global.com/article/function-specific-uncertainty-communication-in-automated-driving/231846)

**Visibility, opacity (alpha) and size as continuous parameters (personalised via Bayesian optimisation)**
- Jansen, Colley, Krauß, Hirschle & Rukzio (2025), CHI '25 (honourable mention). arXiv 2501.06757. **[full text read, arXiv HTML; figures with final values not readable]**
  - Parameters, normalised: other road users' trajectory visibility (boolean via v<0.5 = off), alpha [0.1, 1], size [0.1, 0.6]; ego trajectory visibility, alpha [0.1, 1], size [0.1, 0.6]. There were also semantic-segmentation visibility/alpha and pedestrian-intention visibility/size. Colour was deliberately **not** a parameter ("avoided RGB coloring as parameters"). Length, style and conformality were also not parameters. — [Jansen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)
  - Setup: online, between-subjects, N=117 (Prolific, US). There were six conditions: no visualization, expert design, and several human-in-the-loop MOBO variants. Participants watched a Unity city ride (33 s per iteration; 3 min final route) as an AV passenger. — [Jansen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)
  - Results: the MOBO conditions raised trust (e.g., C4 M=3.94 vs expert design C2 M=3.04), perceived safety and predictability. They lowered cognitive load (C4 M=6.71 vs C2 M=9.76), with no change in acceptance or aesthetics. Results are **not** broken down by the trajectory parameters. In the HITL conditions "many parameters were mostly turned off (v<0.5)". — [Jansen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)
  - Expert design study (n=8 experts, normalised means): trajectory visibility 0.69 (SD 0.18), trajectory alpha 0.61 (0.26), trajectory size 0.46 (0.14), ego trajectory visibility 0.56 (0.26), ego trajectory alpha 0.71 (0.15), ego trajectory size 0.34 (0.10). — [Jansen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)
  - One participant called "the red jerky line and three blue lines" (the trajectories) distracting. — [Jansen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)
- Colley, Jansen, Krauss & Rukzio (2026), "Multi-Session User Experience Assessments of Computationally Optimized Automated Vehicle Functionality Visualizations", arXiv 2607.28552, stated as accepted to AutomotiveUI 2026 (peer review status per arXiv only). Online study, N=74, over three days, with 16 continuous parameters that include the same trajectory and ego-trajectory visibility/alpha/size ranges. **[full text partly read]** — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28552)
  - "Between-participant dispersion of the final designs remained substantial across days rather than collapsing to a single solution". Designs drifted when optimisation continued and stayed near zero drift when the design was frozen. The authors call the per-parameter analyses "preliminary" because logs were incomplete. No trajectory-specific final values were reported in the text. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28552)

**Conformal (world-registered AR) vs non-conformal (planar/icon) path**
- Detjen, Salini, Kronenberger, Geisler & Schneegass (2021), "Towards Transparent Behavior of Automated Vehicles: Design and Evaluation of HUD Concepts to Support System Predictability Through Motion Intent Communication", MobileHCI '21, Art. 19, DOI 10.1145/3447526.3472041. **[abstract/metadata only]**: ACM and ResearchGate returned 403.
  - The study compared an icon-based concept (a planar representation of the future path in the HUD) with an AR concept (a world-fixed path) and a baseline. Both raised UX and trust over the baseline. The AR concept outperformed the icon concept on UX and trust and gave faster, less error-prone take-overs. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3447526.3472041); [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354869065_Towards_Transparent_Behavior_of_Automated_Vehicles_Design_and_Evaluation_of_HUD_Concepts_to_Support_System_Predictability_Through_Motion_Intent_Communication)
  - The authors recommend feedforward of the world-fixed vehicle trajectory to communicate intention. A maneuver queue of upcoming actions was part of the concepts. N=27 appears in a search-engine summary only. **[UNVERIFIED: N and the maneuver-queue detail]** — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3447526.3472041)
- Oliveira, Burns, Luton, Iyer & Birrell (2020), "The influence of system transparency on trust: Evaluating interfaces in a highly automated vehicle", Transportation Research Part F 72, 280–296, DOI 10.1016/j.trf.2020.06.001. N=25 rode in a real fully automated vehicle in an indoor arena. Four interfaces ranged from a simple journey tracker to a windscreen-wide AR overlay of hazards plus **the vehicle's trajectory**. Participants clearly preferred the AR windscreen and an animated representation of the environment, and trust rose over pre-trial values. Some users questioned whether they want this information all the time, and some felt motion sick with the more engaging content. The path is **bundled** with hazard highlighting, so it was not isolated. **[abstract/metadata only]** — [Coventry pure portal](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/the-influence-of-system-transparency-on-trust-evaluating-interfac/); [WRAP accepted version](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/139407)
- Lindemann, Lee & Rigoll (2018), "Catch My Drift: Elevating Situation Awareness for Highly Automated Driving with an Explanatory Windshield Display User Interface", Multimodal Technologies and Interaction 2(4):71 (earlier ISMAR 2018 poster). The WSD combined screen-fixed elements (confidence bar, trip info) with world-registered AR overlays of perception and driving decisions. It improved SA in clear weather (medium effect) and in bad visibility (large effect) in a mixed-reality simulator. The path/decision elements are **bundled**. **[abstract/metadata only]** — [TUM portal](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/supporting-driver-situation-awareness-for-autonomous-urban-drivin/); [MDPI MTI 2(4):71](https://0-www-mdpi-com.brum.beds.ac.uk/2414-4088/2/4/71)
- Schneider, Hois, Rosenstein, Ghellal, Theofanou-Fülbier & Gerlicher (2021), "ExplAIn Yourself! Transparency for Positive UX in Autonomous Driving", CHI '21, Art. 161, DOI 10.1145/3411764.3446647. Live explanations of the future trajectory via an AR WSD or an LED strip increased UX for first-time users. Adding a post-ride smartphone explanation did not. Colley et al. 2023 also say Schneider used "color intensity" rather than hue. **[secondary: summarised in Colley et al. 2022 and Jansen et al. 2025; primary not read]** — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609); [Jansen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)

**Style alternatives: world in miniature, avatar, "ghost car"**
- Häuslschmid, von Bülow, Pfleging & Butz (2017), "Supporting Trust in Autonomous Driving", IUI 2017, DOI 10.1145/3025171.3025198. N=30 sat in a mock-up and watched a real driving scene with HUD overlays. Three conditions were compared: (1) car indicators only (baseline, turn intention only), (2) an animated 3D "world in miniature" (WIM) showing perception, interpretation and actions, and (3) a chauffeur avatar. The WIM increased trust most and gave the strongest feeling of safety and the best UX. The avatar did not differ significantly from the baseline. **[full text read]** — [LMU PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haeuslschmid2017iui/haeuslschmid2017iui.pdf)
- I found **no peer-reviewed occupant-facing study of a "ghost car" / virtual lead vehicle** for AV intention in this search. The "ghost driver" hits concern external, pedestrian-facing studies. — [search result, insideautonomousvehicles](https://insideautonomousvehicles.com/ghost-driver-reveals-what-visual-cues-from-autonomous-cars-may-prove-most-helpful/)

**Colour coding of path state**
- A 2024 online randomised video experiment ("Shared intention and shared awareness for conditional automated driving") showed a curved HUD path for the planned route, with colour distinguishing braking from coasting, plus object-recognition boxes. **[UNVERIFIED: authors, journal, N and results; ScienceDirect returned 403. Only the search-engine summary was seen.]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1538958824001772)

### Inferences
- The best-supported levers for a path overlay are **visibility, opacity, width/size and colour**. These are exactly the parameters Jansen et al. (2025) exposes (minus colour), and they have defined, implementable ranges (alpha 0.1–1, size 0.1–0.6 in normalised units).
- Colley 2023 found that shape, colour and transparency had no effect on trust, workload or safety, and mainly affected understanding. This suggests these properties are low-risk to personalise: preferences can vary without large safety or workload penalties.
- The sign of the trust effect of simply showing the ego path seems to depend on the rendering and context (arrows and gradient in a cluttered multi-layer scene vs a clean line). This makes visibility/opacity of the path a sensible personalised property rather than a fixed design choice.
- Transparency used as an *uncertainty encoding* hurt understanding. That is different from a global opacity setting chosen by the user, which Jansen et al. (2025) treats as a preference.

### Gaps
- No study found here systematically varied **path length / look-ahead time horizon** as an independent variable. Colley 2023 scaled length with speed, and two participants said it was too short.
- No isolated test was found of **animation/motion of the path** (e.g., flowing chevrons vs static). Kunze 2018 suggests animation works well for uncertainty, but I did not verify that it was applied to a path.
- No head-to-head comparison was found of **carpet/lane-wide band vs thin line vs chevrons vs ghost car** for occupants. Only line vs double line vs cone was tested (Colley 2023).
- Final per-participant Jansen et al. (2025) values for trajectory parameters are only in figures (not machine-readable). I could not extract the distributions.
- Detjen et al. 2021 full text (N, exact measures) and the 2024 "shared intention" study could not be accessed.

## Q2. Which manoeuvre/intention cues (icons, text, announcements) have been studied, and how do they compare to path overlays?

### Takeaway
Discrete intention cues (turn indicators, icons, maneuver queues, spoken or visual announcements) have mostly been used as baselines or bundled components. Where they were compared directly with richer path or scene visualisations, the conformal path or WIM won on trust and UX (Detjen 2021; Häuslschmid 2017). Participants in path-only studies nevertheless asked for discrete turning/stopping cues (Colley 2023).

### Cited Findings
- **Icon/planar path vs AR world-fixed path:** both beat the baseline on UX and trust, and AR beat icons on UX, trust and take-over speed and accuracy. **[abstract only]** — [Detjen et al. 2021, MobileHCI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3447526.3472041)
- **Indicator-only (turn intention) vs WIM vs avatar:** the indicator-only baseline gave the lowest trust and the WIM the highest (N=30). — [Häuslschmid et al. 2017, IUI](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haeuslschmid2017iui/haeuslschmid2017iui.pdf)
- **Discrete states requested:** Colley et al. chose a continuous line over discrete states (turning, stopping), but "participants requested this kind of information in their open feedback". — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
- **Upcoming maneuvers for rear-facing occupants:** Wintersberger, Frison, Riener & von Sawitzky (2019), "Fostering User Acceptance and Trust in Fully Automated Vehicles: Evaluating the Potential of Augmented Reality", PRESENCE. Two simulator studies (N=26 normal seating; N=18 rearward seating) found that augmenting otherwise-invisible traffic objects, or presenting upcoming maneuvers when seated backwards, increased acceptance and trust. **[abstract/metadata only; DOI not verified]** — [JKU research portal](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/traffic-augmentation-as-a-means-to-increase-trust-in-automated-dr/)
- **Route indication on an AR HUD:** von Sawitzky, Wintersberger, Riener & Gabbard (2019), "Increasing trust in fully automated driving: route indication on an augmented reality head-up display", PerDis '19. **[UNVERIFIED: levels and results not found; title and authors only, from a dblp/library listing]** — [dblp](https://dblp.org/pid/204/6331)
- **Why/how messages:** Koo et al. (2015) found that "why" (explanatory) information gave the highest trust, while "how" information could cause cognitive overload; combining them gave the safest driving. These were mainly verbal messages, not path graphics. **[secondary, via Colley et al. 2022; primary not read]** — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609)
- **Modality of intent announcements (passenger):** a UMass study with 36 rear-seat passengers in a simulator used auditory, visual or multimodal interfaces that announced perception and intent 5 s before each maneuver, including a lane change. Trust rose pre-to-post in all conditions, and multimodal beat visual-only on trust and perceived transparency. **[UNVERIFIED: authors/venue unclear; appears to be a conference abstract/report]** — [UMass NEUTC](https://www.umass.edu/neutc/media/635/download)
- **Abstract vs futuristic display:** Colley et al. (2021b), cited in Jansen et al. (2025), report that an abstract HUD view "can sufficiently convey critical information". This is not trajectory-specific. **[secondary]** — [Jansen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)

### Inferences
- For a crowdsourced personalisation study, a **"path overlay vs/plus discrete maneuver cue (icon or text)"** property is supported by participants explicitly asking for turning/stopping cues on top of a path. It is also supported by both icon-style and AR-path concepts beating baselines.
- Conformal vs planar presentation favoured AR in the one direct comparison found. It may be less suitable as a personalised continuous parameter, and fits better as a fixed design choice or a categorical option.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that compares text labels (e.g., "Turning left") with icons with path overlays for occupants on trust/SA in one design.
- Timing/lead time of announcements as a display property was found only in the unverified UMass work (5 s).

## Q3. Participants, methods and the evidence for individual differences (personalisation candidates) vs consensus/no-effect properties

### Takeaway
Most path studies are online and video-based (N≈117–280, Prolific) and take a passenger viewpoint. A few are simulator or on-track studies with N≈25–30. Several sources report strong individual differences: on whether to show a visualisation at all, on opacity/visibility of trajectories, and on how final optimised designs disperse. Shape, colour and transparency of the path showed **no effect** on trust, workload or perceived safety (Colley 2023). The one fairly clear consensus is that a single line is understood better than a double line or cone, and that hue beats transparency for uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
**Participant samples (summary)**
- Colley 2022: online video, front-passenger view, N=216. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609)
- Colley 2023: online Unity video, N=280, plus online real-world video, N=238. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
- Jansen et al. (2025): online interactive Unity ride, N=117, passenger. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)
- Colley et al. 2026 (multi-session): online, N=74, three days. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28552)
- Häuslschmid 2017: lab with real driving footage on a HUD, N=30. — [PDF](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haeuslschmid2017iui/haeuslschmid2017iui.pdf)
- Oliveira 2020: real AV in an indoor arena, N=25. — [Coventry](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/the-influence-of-system-transparency-on-trust-evaluating-interfac/)
- Currano et al. (2021), "Little Road Driving HUD: Heads-Up Display Complexity Influences Drivers' Perceptions of Automated Vehicles", CHI '21, DOI 10.1145/3411764.3445575: online, N=298, three HUD complexity conditions (none / minimal / complex). Path content of the HUD **[UNVERIFIED]**. — [unpaywall/DOI](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3411764.3445575)

**Individual differences (support for personalisation)**
- Jansen et al. 2025 expert study: SD>0.18 (diverse views) for **trajectory alpha** (SD 0.26) and **ego trajectory visibility** (SD 0.26); trajectory size had SD≤0.18 ("fair alignment"). HITL conditions had wider intervals than the expert design, and "individual differences persist". — [Jansen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06757)
- Multi-session: dispersion of final designs between participants "remained substantial across days rather than collapsing to a single solution". — [Colley et al. 2026 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28552)
- Häuslschmid 2017: "participants did not agree on the need of a visualization". Some felt safer with one and others without. The authors conclude "a one-fits-all visualization might not be sufficient". Opinions on the same driving style also split between aggressive and defensive. — [Häuslschmid et al. 2017](https://www.medien.ifi.lmu.de/pubdb/publications/pub/haeuslschmid2017iui/haeuslschmid2017iui.pdf)
- Currano 2021: effects depended on scene dynamics and participants' self-reported driving style, and the authors argue the HUD should adapt to both. Including a HUD *reduced* SA compared with no HUD. **[abstract + secondary via Colley 2022]** — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445575)
- Oliveira 2020: some users questioned seeing the information all the time, and some got motion sick from engaging content. **[abstract only]** — [Coventry](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/the-influence-of-system-transparency-on-trust-evaluating-interfac/)
- Colley 2023: the uncertainty colour on the ego path reassured some participants and made others doubt the AV. — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
- Colley 2022 recommends letting users toggle each visualisation layer. — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609)

**No-effect / consensus properties**
- Shape × colour × transparency (13 conditions, N=280): no effect on trust, perceived safety or workload, and no condition beat the no-visualization baseline. — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)
- Consensus-ish findings: a **single line** is better than a double line or cone for understanding, **hue** is better than transparency for uncertainty (Colley 2023 and Kunze 2018 agree), and **AR world-fixed** is better than a planar icon path (Detjen 2021, one study). — [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408); [Kunze et al. 2018](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/Augmented_reality_displays_for_communicating_uncertainty_information_in_automated_driving/9338876/files/16947521.pdf); [Detjen et al. 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3447526.3472041)
- Ego-path presence had no effect on mental workload in either Ulm study. Workload and clutter came from other agents' predicted trajectories. — [Colley et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3534609); [Colley et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1145/3631408)

### Inferences
- Strongest candidates for preference-based personalisation in the path/intention family:
  - **(a) path visibility/opacity**: the expert SD was high, the trust effects were inconsistent, and users split on wanting it at all.
  - **(b) path width/size**: it is in Jansen et al. (2025), with less disagreement but still variable.
  - **(c) colour/uncertainty colouring**: some users were reassured and others worried. Note it was excluded from Jansen et al. (2025) for RGB-parameterisation reasons.
  - **(d) look-ahead length**: untested as an independent variable, with some "too short" complaints.
- Properties with a near-consensus (line vs double line/cone; conformal vs planar; transparency as an uncertainty code) could be fixed rather than personalised.
- Because Jansen et al. (2025) already personalised trajectory alpha/size/visibility with MOBO, a new study adding **length/horizon** and **style (line vs chevrons vs band)** would extend rather than replicate it.

### Gaps
- No study was found that reports the *distribution* of preferred path opacity/width/length across individuals in numbers. The Jansen et al. (2025) figures were not readable in text.
- Driver-vs-passenger differences in path preferences were not directly compared in any study found. Most online studies take a passenger view, and Detjen 2021 is a take-over (driver) context.
- A claim from a search summary that an "AR for passengers" simulator study (N=18) found trust depends on driving decisions and road-user info more than on location-specific info could not be traced to a primary source **[UNVERIFIED]**. — [search summary only; possibly from the i-com review](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/icom-2021-0029/html?lang=en)
